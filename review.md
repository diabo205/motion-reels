## 1. Fix the objective errors in the spec first

**Three different duration ceilings, and the math doesn't reconcile.** The doc says 45.0s–58.5s, "≤ 3,500 frames", and a "≤59.0s ceiling" in the same table row. At 60 FPS, 3,500 frames is 58.33s, and 58.5s is 3,510 frames. Since the audit checks frames ≤ 3,500, your real ceiling is 58.33s. Derive everything from one constant (`MAX_FRAMES = 3500`) and let the docs, audit, and compiler all read it.

**The Content ID rationale is wrong.** Content ID doesn't block or claim based on duration — a matched music recording gets claimed whether the video is 30s or 3 minutes. Separately, YouTube has allowed Shorts up to **3 minutes since October 15, 2024** ([YouTube's announcement](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/tall-updates-coming-to-shorts), [Help Center](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)). Keeping 45–58.5s is still a defensible *creative* constraint (retention pacing), but reframe it that way — the real Content ID mitigation is music sourcing, which the doc never mentions. Record the BGM's license/clearance ID in the project manifest.

**"16–22 cuts per reel" only holds at the slow end of your own pacing range.** At 2.0s/scene, a 58s reel is ~29 cuts. Either tighten the pacing floor to ~2.4s or widen the stated cut range to 16–29.

**Frame-drift policy is unstated.** 104 BPM = 34.615 frames/beat — non-integer. If `extract_beat_grid.py` rounds each beat interval independently, error accumulates (~0.4 frames/beat → audibly visible desync after a few bars, and your slam timing lives or dies on this). The correct policy: compute cumulative beat positions in float, then round absolute frame numbers once. Also worth defining what scene cuts snap to — right now the 2.0–2.8s window and the beat grid are described as parallel systems with no stated relationship.

**Per-scene rendering conflicts with "particles run unbroken across cuts."** `render_scene_clip.js` produces isolated clips, but a continuous emitter can't be consistent across independently rendered clips unless it's seeded by *global* frame time, not scene-relative time. Two-part fix: (a) seed all particle randomness with `(emitterId, globalFrame)` so any render window reproduces the same field; (b) render the master as parallel per-scene base layers + one full-length overlay pass (particles, grade, audio) composited with ffmpeg. That gives you both parallelism *and* continuity — and it means the master is literally a concat of the scene clips you already QA'd, eliminating the "iteration path" vs "final render path" divergence you currently have.

## 2. Your typography ignores your own beat grid

This is the biggest conceptual gap. The doc's headline claim is "beat-quantized" timelines, yet the Red Payoff Line slams at a **hardcoded 0.5s (Frame 30) in every scene**. After 16 scenes that's a metronome viewers predict, and it's disconnected from the music. Sync the slam to the first detected downbeat *after* each scene's start — you already extracted the grid; consume it. Same for SFX placement: bias transient SFX 2–3 frames *early* rather than late (audio-leading-video is perceived as sync; video-leading-audio is not — the ~45ms perceptual asymmetry).

Also, the container-width formula `text.length × fontSize × 0.72 + 80px` is a monospace heuristic applied to Montserrat Black, a wide grotesque where `I` and `W` differ enormously, and it's silently wrong for any future localization (CJK advance width is ~1.0 em, not 0.72). Measure real advance widths from the vendored font file (opentype.js or headless canvas `measureText`) — you get exactness for free and the constant stops being a font-locked magic number. While you're in there: mandate a max of 2 lines (6–10 words can easily wrap to 3+ at <40 chars/line), a tracking spec for ALL-CAPS (+2–6% letter-spacing — all-caps without tracking reads as a wall), and a minimum size floor (~64px setup / ~96px payoff at 1080 width for phone legibility). None of these are currently specified.

And one subtle but high-value bug: **frame 0 is your worst frame.** The White Setup Line enters with a 150ms *blur-to-sharp* at 0.0s, meaning the poster/thumbnail frame — the frame feeds and cover pickers show — is smeared. Either start sharp and animate in, or export a designed poster PNG as a pipeline artifact.

## 3. Turn every "banned" into a check — this is an agent pipeline, so drift is guaranteed

The README is full of standards with no enforcement: crash zooms banned, cursive/typewriter banned, zero photo-pasting, locked palettes, mandatory micro-motion per scene, 6–10 word budget. The `--audit-stills` check only verifies geometry and duration. For an agent-driven system, unenforced standards are standards that silently disappear within weeks of iterations. Cheap to build, huge payoff:

- **Palette lint**: parse generated `*Vectors.tsx` / SVG for hex fills not in the locked palette set.
- **Font lint**: flag any font family outside Montserrat Black / Inter ExtraBold.
- **Photo-paste lint**: flag `<img>` / `<Image>` / `ImageStaticFile` in vector components — this enforces your own "archival photos as reference only" rule automatically.
- **Micro-motion check**: frame-diff a scene's first and last second; near-zero delta = missing secondary loop.
- **Audit output as JSON**, not just PNGs: `{sceneId, rule, expected, actual, frame}`. This is the feedback signal your agents can loop on — right now a failing audit produces images only a human can interpret, which wastes the closed loop. Add an auto-fix mode that regenerates only the failing scene.
- **CI smoke render**: one scene at 540×960 @ 10fps on every PR, plus JSON-schema validation of all timelines.

## 4. Platform delivery is thinner than the render pipeline deserves

- **No loudness spec at all.** All three platforms normalize audio; an uncontrolled mix comes out either squashed or whisper-quiet. Add a mastering step (two-pass ffmpeg `loudnorm` to −14 LUFS integrated, −1 dBTP) and probe it in the audit. You know the exact slam frames — derive BGM ducking (−6 to −9 dB under impacts) from the timeline automatically.
- **Safe zones are hardcoded for a union of UIs that changes.** Make them a per-platform *profile* (config), render the conservative union by default, and note that Instagram crops 9:16 to 4:5 in the feed grid — if IG feed reach matters, keep text inside the center 1080×1350 region.
- **You're sitting on metadata and throwing it away.** The timeline JSON contains every word, timestamp, and fact. Auto-emit per-project: title (≤100 chars), description, hashtags, alt-text, and an SRT — the last one is a genuine accessibility win and costs nothing since timing is already frame-exact. Cover/thumbnail export fits here too.
- **PowerShell locks you to Windows.** `render_pipeline.ps1` blocks CI (ubuntu runners), Mac collaborators, and — the real prize — Remotion Lambda for parallel cloud rendering. You already have Node scripts; make Node the pipeline and keep PS1 as a thin wrapper.
- **`current_reel.json` is implicit global state.** None of your example commands take a project slug — the active project is whatever file happens to be in `src/timelines/`. Make the project an explicit CLI argument everywhere, and add a per-project manifest (audio hash, font versions, Remotion version, palette ID) so renders are reproducible. Pin and vendor the fonts — a Google Fonts CDN update silently changes metrics and breaks your width math.

## 5. Creative-layer risks the spec doesn't cover

- **Archetype fatigue.** With 90% of scenes drawn from six archetypes, viewers will pattern-match your template within ~5 videos of a channel. Add a rotation/history lint (no repeated opening archetype across consecutive reels) and parameterized variants per archetype. And I'd add a seventh archetype that's conspicuously absent: **QUOTE_CARD** — a primary-source quote reveal (intercept, telegram, diary). For historical content it's the strongest credibility *and* retention device there is, and you already have `KineticTextStack`.
- **The five-act table hardcodes scene counts and timings** (19 scenes, acts ending at 46.0s — also oddly *below* your own 45s floor's typical use). Express acts as proportions so they stretch to any duration in the window.
- **No loop/CTA spec.** Rewatch rate is heavily rewarded; an optional seamless loop mode (last frame flows into first) or a designed end-card with CTA placed inside safe zones is a cheap structural win.
- **No editorial layer.** This engine renders casualty tolls over synthesized explosions for real, recent mass-casualty events (e.g. peshawar-2009). That needs two things the schema lacks: per-metric **source citations** (`claim: {text, source, url}` — a fact-check gate before render; one wrong number in the comments kills a history channel's algorithmic performance), and a **sensitivity flag** that can steer presentation (somber palette variant, no triumphant slams on casualty scenes) — both for taste and for platform moderation, which limits graphic content regardless of accuracy.
- **No outcome telemetry.** The whole point of building this programmatically is that you can close the loop: log per-scene retention (YouTube retention graph, TikTok's per-second drop) against archetype/pacing choices and feed it back as priors. Even a simple A/B on two hooks would make this the only pipeline of its kind that *learns*. This is the highest-leverage strategic upgrade in the whole list.
